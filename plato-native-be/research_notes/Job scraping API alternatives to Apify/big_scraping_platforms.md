# General-Purpose Scraping Platforms as Apify Alternatives for Job-Board Scraping

Apify baseline for comparison: ~$0.0007–$0.005 per job result (per-record pricing).

## Do Bright Data, Oxylabs, ScraperAPI, ScrapingBee, PhantomBuster, Coresignal, and Proxycurl offer pre-built products for these 12 job boards, or would a custom scraper against generic infrastructure be required?

### Takeaway
Only Bright Data and Oxylabs have dedicated, pre-built dataset/scraper products for job boards, and even they only cover a subset (LinkedIn, Indeed, Glassdoor, and a generic "Job Postings" dataset that aggregates ZipRecruiter/Dice/Monster). Coresignal and Proxycurl are LinkedIn-only specialists (Proxycurl has shut down). ScraperAPI, ScrapingBee, and PhantomBuster have no job-board-specific dataset/API product — they would require a custom scraper built on their generic unblocking/proxy or browser-automation infrastructure. None of the seven vendors appear to offer dedicated products for Talent.com, Jobillico, JobBank Canada, BuiltIn, Handshake, or Wuzzuf.

### Cited Findings
- Bright Data has a dedicated "LinkedIn Jobs" scraper/dataset product page and a separate "Indeed Datasets" / "Indeed Job Posting Dataset" product page — [LinkedIn Datasets](https://brightdata.com/products/datasets/linkedin), [Indeed Job Posting Dataset](https://brightdata.com/products/datasets/indeed/job-posting), [Indeed Scraper API](https://brightdata.com/products/web-scraper/indeed)
- Bright Data's "Job Postings Dataset" (sold via Databricks Marketplace) is a multi-source aggregation "sourced from various websites such as Indeed, Ziprecruiter, LinkedIn, Monster, and Dice," covering fields like job title, company, salary range, remote flag, and location — [Databricks Marketplace listing](https://marketplace.databricks.com/details/d19c773f-ffc3-4f0c-825b-3d9076192e58/Bright-Data_Job-Postings-Dataset)
- Bright Data also has a dedicated "Google Jobs Scraper API" as part of its SERP API product line — [Google Jobs Scraper API](https://brightdata.com/products/serp-api/google-search/jobs)
- Oxylabs sells a "Job Posting Dataset" product explicitly covering Indeed and Glassdoor (plus StackShare for tech jobs); the page does not mention LinkedIn, ZipRecruiter, Dice, Talent.com, Jobillico, JobBank Canada, BuiltIn, Handshake, or Wuzzuf as covered sources — [Oxylabs Job Posting Dataset](https://oxylabs.io/products/datasets/job-posting)
- Oxylabs separately markets a "Job Scraper API" (a generic-scraper-based product, not a pre-collected dataset) that it says supports "multiple job boards including Glassdoor, Indeed, and ZipRecruiter" — [Oxylabs Jobs Scraper API](https://oxylabs.io/products/scraper-api/web/jobs-scraper)
- Coresignal offers a "Jobs API" / "Jobs Data API" described as a large-scale jobs database, but all identified sourcing/marketing centers on LinkedIn and professional-network job data rather than the other 11 named boards — [Coresignal Jobs Data API](https://coresignal.com/solutions/jobs-data-api/)
- Proxycurl offered a "Job Search" endpoint (LinkedIn Jobs specifically) priced per credit, but Proxycurl shut down / stopped operating for new use as of around July 2025, per multiple secondary sources — [Proxycurl Pricing page](https://nubela.co/proxycurl/pricing.html), [Proxycurl Review — shutdown](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/proxycurl-review), [Proxycurl founder blog on 2026 status](https://nubela.co/blog/what-is-proxycurl-api-now-in-2026-im-the-founder/)
- PhantomBuster has a "LinkedIn Job Post Scraper" / "LinkedIn Job Scraper" automation ("Phantom") that extracts job title, location, description, company name, and posted date from LinkedIn job posts, but this is a LinkedIn-only browser-automation tool, not a dataset/API product, and no equivalent Phantoms were found for the other 11 boards — [LinkedIn Job Post Scraper](https://landing.phantombuster.com/linkedin-job-post-scraper), [LinkedIn Job Scraper automation](https://phantombuster.com/automations/linkedin/6772788738377011/linkedin-job-scraper)
- ScraperAPI's pricing/product page lists industry-specific "Data Collection" solutions for e-commerce, market research, real estate, and SERP data, but no LinkedIn/Indeed/Glassdoor/job-board-specific product was found — [ScraperAPI Pricing](https://www.scraperapi.com/pricing/)
- No dedicated job-board product was found for ScrapingBee in search results; its offering is a generic scraping/rendering API billed by credits — [ScrapingBee credit system](https://help.scrapingbee.com/en/article/credit-system-explained-1h2ackp/)
- For Talent.com and Jobillico specifically, search results returned only Apify-hosted actors (multiple third-party Apify actors from different Apify creators) — no Bright Data, Oxylabs, ScraperAPI, ScrapingBee, PhantomBuster, Coresignal, or Proxycurl product was found covering these two boards — [Talent.com Jobs Scraper (Apify)](https://apify.com/crawlerbros/talent-com-jobs-scraper/api), [Jobillico Jobs Search Scraper (Apify)](https://apify.com/jobsapi/jobillico-jobs-search-scraper/api)
- No vendor-specific product was found for JobBank Canada, BuiltIn, Handshake, or Wuzzuf across any of the seven researched vendors in this round of searching.

### Inferences
- For LinkedIn, Indeed, and Glassdoor, Bright Data and Oxylabs are viable "pre-built" alternatives to Apify actors. For ZipRecruiter and Dice, only Bright Data's bundled multi-source "Job Postings Dataset" and Oxylabs' generic "Job Scraper API" claim coverage — neither offers a dedicated ZipRecruiter or Dice-only dataset with its own pricing.
- For Google Jobs, Bright Data's SERP API product family (with a dedicated Google Jobs endpoint) is the only true pre-built option found among the seven; ScraperAPI/ScrapingBee would require building a custom Google Jobs scraper on their generic SERP/rendering infrastructure.
- For Talent.com, Jobillico, JobBank Canada, BuiltIn, Handshake, and Wuzzuf, all seven vendors would most likely require a fully custom scraper built on their generic proxy/unblocker or browser-automation infrastructure — no dedicated dataset/actor-equivalent product appears to exist for these boards at any of the seven vendors.

### Gaps
- Could not directly confirm (vendor pricing/product pages were not directly crawlable in full) whether Bright Data or Oxylabs have ANY product, even partial, touching Talent.com, Jobillico, JobBank Canada, BuiltIn, Handshake, or Wuzzuf — searches returned no matches, which is suggestive but not conclusive proof of absence.
- Could not verify whether Coresignal's Jobs API pulls from job boards beyond LinkedIn (e.g., multi-source jobs) — Coresignal is also known for a "Multi-Source Jobs API" (mentioned in passing in pricing search results) but its exact source coverage (which boards feed it) was not confirmed in this research pass.
- Could not confirm PhantomBuster has any Phantom automations for job boards other than LinkedIn.

## What is the actual price per 1,000 results or per API call for job-listing data specifically (not generic proxy/bandwidth pricing)?

### Takeaway
Where dedicated job-data products exist, current (2026) per-record pricing clusters roughly around $0.75–$2.50 per 1,000 records for Bright Data and Oxylabs dataset/SERP products, and roughly $0.005–$0.20 per record for Coresignal depending on plan tier — all meaningfully more expensive than Apify's stated $0.0007–$0.005/record baseline, often by 1–2 orders of magnitude at the lower end of Apify's range.

### Cited Findings
- **Bright Data — LinkedIn Jobs / general LinkedIn dataset**: one-time purchase pricing starts at $250 for 100K records (~$2.50/1,000), dropping to $2,000/1M (~$2.00/1,000) and $5,000/5M (~$1.00/1,000); subscription refresh plans offer up to 25% off (bi-annual), 50% off (quarterly), or 80% off (monthly refresh) — [Bright Data Pricing/Datasets](https://brightdata.com/pricing/datasets), secondary summary via [ApiSerpent Bright Data LinkedIn pricing](https://apiserpent.com/blog/brightdata-linkedin-pricing-explained)

- **Bright Data — Indeed dataset**: flat $250 per 100,000 records (~$2.50/1,000) for the Indeed job-posting dataset, with daily/weekly/monthly/custom refresh options and the ability to buy a subset of fields to cut cost — [Indeed Datasets](https://brightdata.com/products/datasets/indeed), [Indeed Job Posting Dataset](https://brightdata.com/products/datasets/indeed/job-posting)

Plan tiers: Mini $49/mo (2,500 credits), Starter $199/mo, Pro $499/mo, Growth $1,000/mo, Premium $1,500/mo, Scale $3,000/mo, Elite $5,000/mo (10,000,000 credits) — [Coresignal Pricing](https://coresignal.com/pricing/), [Coresignal Docs Pricing](https://docs.coresignal.com/pricing)

- Generic-infrastructure vendors (ScraperAPI, ScrapingBee) would likely cost far more than Apify per job record once anti-bot bypass / JS rendering / stealth proxies (all typically required for LinkedIn, Indeed, Glassdoor, ZipRecruiter) are factored in — ScraperAPI's ~$3–$8.49/1,000 requests and ScrapingBee's ~$14.70/1,000 stealth pages are both well above Apify's $0.0007–$0.005/record baseline, and that is priced per successful HTTP request/page, not per parsed job record (a single job listing might need one page load plus possibly detail-page loads, further inflating true cost-per-record).
- Coresignal is the cheapest of the specialist LinkedIn-data vendors at higher volume tiers (secondary estimates of $0.005–$0.03/record at $800–$1,500/month spend) but is markedly more expensive than Apify at low volume/entry tiers ($0.13–$0.20/record cited by one source).

### Gaps
- Could not obtain primary-source (vendor pricing-page) confirmation of the exact per-1,000 LinkedIn Jobs and Indeed Jobs rates attributed to Bright Data's blog post ($1.50/1,000 PAYG, $0.75–$0.98/1,000 subscription) — this came from a fetched summary of a Bright Data blog post rather than the brightdata.com/pricing/datasets page itself, so it should be treated as reasonably reliable but not verified against the canonical pricing table.

## For platforms with no dedicated product (from these 7 vendors), what would a generic Scraper API/unblocker product cost per request?

### Takeaway
For boards without a dedicated dataset (ZipRecruiter, Dice, Talent.com, Jobillico, JobBank Canada, BuiltIn, Handshake, Wuzzuf), the realistic fallback is a generic scraper/unblocker product billed per successful request (not per job record), and that unit cost is substantially higher on a like-for-like basis than Apify's per-record pricing: roughly $0.25–$1.60 per 1,000 requests for Oxylabs' Web Scraper API (varies heavily by target-site difficulty), roughly $3–$8.49 per 1,000 requests for ScraperAPI, and roughly $10–$14.70+ per 1,000 requests for ScrapingBee when stealth/JS-rendering is required (as most modern job boards with anti-bot protection would require).

### Cited Findings
- Oxylabs' generic Web Scraper API billing unit is explicitly "results," defined as "successfully scraped content entities" — pricing starts from $1.6/1,000 results at entry level, with pay-as-you-go rates from $0.50 down to $0.25/1,000 as commitment rises, and plan-and-target-dependent rates as low as $0.40/1,000 (Amazon) up to $1.25/1,000 (JS rendering) on the $249/month Advanced plan — [Oxylabs Web Scraper API pricing](https://oxylabs.io/products/scraper-api/web/pricing), [Oxylabs billing docs](https://developers.oxylabs.io/help-center/billing-and-payments/how-does-web-scraper-api-pricing-work)
- ScraperAPI bills per request/credit: minimum reported $3/1,000 requests (no rendering), $7/1,000 (rendered pages); credits are 1 for basic HTML, 5–10 for JS rendering, 25 for "premium domains"; independent third-party benchmarks found real-world average costs of $3.20/1,000 (Scrapeway, Aug 2026) to $8.49/1,000 (Scrape.do analysis) — [ScraperAPI credits & request costs docs](https://docs.scraperapi.com/getting-started/quick-start/credits-and-requests-costs)
- ScrapingBee bills per credit, with credit cost scaling by feature use: 10 credits (Classic), 20 (Premium proxy), 80 (Stealth proxy, typically needed for heavily-protected sites), +5 for AI-assisted extraction; a cited real-world example (stealth + JS rendering) works out to roughly $14.70 per 1,000 actual pages scraped on the mid-tier plan — [ScrapingBee credit system explainer](https://help.scrapingbee.com/en/article/credit-system-explained-1h2ackp/)
- Both ScraperAPI's and ScrapingBee's pricing is explicitly billed per successful HTTP request/page, not per parsed/structured job record — meaning actual cost-per-job-listing would be higher still once list-page + detail-page fetches and any custom parsing logic are accounted for (parsing/normalization is not included the way it is in a purpose-built dataset product).

### Inferences
- None of ScraperAPI, ScrapingBee, or (for the non-dataset boards) Oxylabs' generic API include built-in parsing/structuring of job-specific fields the way Bright Data's/Oxylabs' dedicated job datasets do — engineering effort to build and maintain board-specific parsers (and handle each board's anti-bot defenses) would be a real additional cost on top of the raw per-request price, which is not the case with Apify's pre-built job-board actors.
- On a per-request (not per-record) basis, Oxylabs' generic Web Scraper API is the cheapest of the three "generic infrastructure" options for boards lacking a dedicated product, but even its floor price (~$0.25–$1.6/1,000) is still meaningfully above Apify's stated per-record floor ($0.0007/1,000 = $0.70... note: $0.0007/record = $0.70/1,000 records, so Apify's floor is actually comparable to or cheaper than Oxylabs' floor; Apify's ceiling of $0.005/record = $5/1,000 is within Oxylabs' generic API's upper range).

### Gaps
- Could not find PhantomBuster's per-request or per-1,000-result generic pricing normalized in the same unit (it bills by execution-hour/Phantom-slot, not by request count), so it cannot be directly compared to the other generic-infrastructure vendors on a $/1,000-requests basis.
- Could not confirm whether any of these generic-infrastructure vendors offer bundled discounts or specific plan tiers optimized for high-volume job-board scraping (e.g., negotiated enterprise rates) beyond list pricing.

## Do these vendors publish ToS/legal risk notes about scraping LinkedIn/Indeed/Glassdoor specifically?

### Takeaway
Both Bright Data and Oxylabs publish public content addressing the legality of scraping, anchored heavily around the hiQ Labs v. LinkedIn and Meta v. Bright Data case law; the consistent message across both vendors is that scraping publicly available, logged-out data is broadly defensible in the US, while logged-in/authenticated scraping that violates a platform's terms of service carries meaningfully more legal risk.

### Cited Findings
- In *Meta Platforms v. Bright Data* (N.D. Cal., decided ~January 2024), the court granted Bright Data summary judgment; Bright Data scrapes Meta's platforms only in a "logged-off" (no active Meta account) state, and the court found Meta's ToS "survival" clause purporting to prohibit scraping of public data in perpetuity was unenforceable — the ruling built directly on the same judge's earlier hiQ precedent — [Quinn Emanuel client alert on Meta v. Bright Data](https://www.quinnemanuel.com/the-firm/news-events/client-alert-meta-v-bright-data-significant-decision-for-web-scraping-industry/), [Quinn Emanuel Q&A PDF](https://www.quinnemanuel.com/media/bq0josrj/bright-data-questions-answered-and-unanswered-45.pdf)
- The emergent legal principle cited across sources: "logged-out public scraping is defensible; logged-in scraping against accepted terms is not" — [Farella Braun + Martel summary](https://www.fbm.com/publications/major-decision-affects-law-of-scraping-and-online-data-collection-meta-platforms-v-bright-data/)
- hiQ Labs, Inc. v. LinkedIn Corp. is the foundational precedent (public-data scraping does not violate the CFAA), but hiQ itself ultimately settled in December 2022, agreeing to a permanent injunction and a $500,000 judgment forcing it to stop scraping LinkedIn — despite having won on the core CFAA question — [Lection blog: hiQ Labs v. LinkedIn explained](https://www.lection.app/blogs/hiq-labs-vs-linkedin-case-explained)
- Oxylabs publishes its own public legal-guidance content: a blog post "Is Web Scraping Legal?" and a parallel help-center doc "Is web scraping legal?" — both apparently reiterating that scraping publicly available LinkedIn/Indeed/Glassdoor-type data is generally defensible in the US per hiQ v. LinkedIn, while cautioning to "use public data only and avoid credential-based scraping to stay compliant," and to respect ToS, avoid impersonating users, and avoid bypassing security measures — [Oxylabs blog: Is Web Scraping Legal?](https://oxylabs.io/blog/is-web-scraping-legal), [Oxylabs help center: Is web scraping legal?](https://developers.oxylabs.io/help-center/products-and-features/is-web-scraping-legal)
- Bright Data publishes a blog post "Best LinkedIn Scrapers in 2026" that, per its title/context, addresses LinkedIn scraping tooling, though the specific legal-risk language within it was not directly captured in this research pass — [Bright Data: Best LinkedIn Scraping Tools](https://brightdata.com/blog/web-data/best-linkedin-scraping-tools)

### Inferences
- Bright Data has the strongest direct legal precedent behind it (it was literally the defendant in the key 2024 ruling), which it can point to as evidence its logged-out scraping model is defensible — this is a differentiator worth noting for Plato if legal risk tolerance is a decision factor.
- Neither Bright Data nor Oxylabs appears to publish board-by-board legal risk breakdowns specific to Indeed or Glassdoor (as opposed to LinkedIn/Meta); their public legal content is framed generically around "public vs. logged-in data" rather than platform-specific ToS analysis.

### Gaps
