# Switching from Apify rarely pays off

Across the twelve job boards Plato scrapes — LinkedIn, Indeed, Glassdoor, ZipRecruiter, Dice, Google Jobs, Talent.com, Jobillico, JobBank Canada, BuiltIn, Handshake, and Wuzzuf — no single vendor category reliably beats Apify's $0.0007–$0.005 per-record baseline once real coverage, access, and freshness constraints are applied. The one unambiguous win is **DataForSEO's Google Jobs SERP endpoint**, which prices out at roughly **$0.06–$0.12 per 1,000 results** ([DataForSEO Google Jobs pricing](https://dataforseo.com/pricing/serp/google-jobs-serp-api)) against an estimated **$2.20 per 1,000** for the current Apify Google Jobs actor — an order-of-magnitude saving, with the tradeoff being a turnaround time of up to 45 minutes on the cheapest queue. Outside that, the picture is bleak for switching: official partner APIs from LinkedIn, Indeed, ZipRecruiter, and Glassdoor are gatekept behind enterprise sales cycles and five- to six-figure minimum spends; free aggregator APIs (Adzuna, Jooble, Careerjet, JSearch, USAJobs) either cap out at trivial volumes or have no confirmed overlap with the specific 12 boards; and dedicated scraping-as-a-service platforms (Bright Data, Oxylabs, Coresignal, ScraperAPI, ScrapingBee, PhantomBuster, Proxycurl) collectively build pre-made products for only three of the twelve boards — LinkedIn, Indeed, and Glassdoor — leaving the other nine to custom-built scrapers on generic infrastructure that typically cost more than Apify's community actors once anti-bot bypass and JS rendering are factored in. The two genuinely low-risk, low-effort wins that don't require a new vendor relationship are switching two of Plato's five niche-board Apify actors (Jobillico, BuiltIn) to cheaper same-platform alternatives, and modeling an Apify subscription-tier upgrade against Plato's real monthly volume.

## DataForSEO undercuts Apify by an order of magnitude on Google Jobs, but only if 45-minute-old data is tolerable

Google shut down its only official Jobs query API in May 2021, and Google's own developer documentation confirms no public API exists today to retrieve Google for Jobs results programmatically ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/job-posting)) — every provider in this category, including the current Apify actor, is technically an unofficial scraper of Google's search results page. Among the SERP-API wrappers, pricing diverges sharply by provider and by speed tier. SerpApi bills per search (up to 10 results per search) across tiers from Free (250 searches) through Infrastructure ($2,750/mo, 500K searches); normalized to $/1,000 job results this ranges from **$2.50 at the entry Starter tier down to $0.55 at the top self-serve tier** ([SerpApi Pricing](https://serpapi.com/pricing)), meaning SerpApi is *more* expensive than Apify's current Google Jobs actor (~$2.20/1,000) at low volume and only pulls ahead once monthly spend reaches the Developer tier ($75/mo) or above. DataForSEO's Google Jobs SERP API is priced very differently: its Standard queue costs **$0.0006 per SERP call (10 results)** — about **$0.06 per 1,000 results** — but carries a target turnaround of up to 45 minutes with "extended processing" possible beyond that; its Priority queue costs **$0.0012 per SERP call (~$0.12 per 1,000 results)** with turnaround of roughly one minute ([DataForSEO Google Jobs SERP API pricing](https://dataforseo.com/pricing/serp/google-jobs-serp-api)). Even the fast Priority queue is roughly **18x cheaper than Apify's current per-record cost** for the same board. A third figure — a "Live mode" at $0.002/SERP ($0.20/1,000) — appears only in a secondary aggregator source and was not confirmed against DataForSEO's own pricing page, so it should be treated as unverified pending a direct check. RapidAPI's JSearch product is a related but distinct option: it is confirmed to be a wrapper around Google for Jobs results (not an independent source), priced at $25/mo for 10,000 requests (~$0.0025/request before accounting for results per request), putting it in a similar cost range to SerpApi rather than to DataForSEO ([OpenWeb Ninja JSearch listing](https://www.openwebninja.com/api/jsearch)). Because DataForSEO, SerpApi, and JSearch are all ultimately wrapping the same underlying Google index, none of them escapes the shared staleness risk that a listing pulled off Google's SERP may lag the source board's own removal or update — a limitation identical in kind to what Apify's own Google Jobs actor already carries, so DataForSEO's cost advantage isn't offset by a coverage or freshness downgrade relative to the status quo.

## Official partner APIs are gatekept behind enterprise contracts, not pricing

For the platforms with the largest volume — LinkedIn and Indeed — the official API path is not merely more expensive than Apify, it is largely inaccessible to a company at Plato's scale. LinkedIn's Recruiter/Talent Solutions API requires an existing incorporated-company relationship with LinkedIn Talent Solutions, a review process commonly reported at 3–6 months with an approval rate under 10%, and a minimum annual contract commonly cited in the **$50,000–$100,000** range ([Getphyllo LinkedIn API guide](https://www.getphyllo.com/post/linkedin-api-ultimate-guide-on-linkedin-api-integration), [Microsoft Learn — LinkedIn API access](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/shared/authentication/getting-access)) — figures sourced from secondary developer guides rather than a published LinkedIn price sheet, but directionally consistent across multiple sources. Indeed's self-serve Publisher API/XML feed was **shut down in 2023** with no self-serve replacement; the only remaining paths are a partner-gated employer/ATS API ecosystem (irrelevant for a job-data consumer) or a separate NDA-bound enterprise data-partnership program reportedly carrying six-figure minimum commitments ([Indeed Partner Docs release notes](https://docs.indeed.com/release-notes); six-figure claim per [JobsPipe's Indeed API guide](https://jobspipe.dev/blog/indeed-api-guide), unconfirmed by a primary Indeed source). ZipRecruiter and Glassdoor both run official Jobs APIs, but both are scoped to ATS vendors and staffing-platform partners under a signed agreement rather than offered as a self-serve read API for job-search products ([ZipRecruiter Partner Platform](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/partner/documentation/job-api/), [Glassdoor Developer — Jobs API](https://www.glassdoor.com/developer/jobsApiActions.htm)). Handshake is a partial exception: it does publish a documented, read-only "EDU API" that exposes job/posting data, but access is granted per-institution to a university's Career Services office via a Handshake Relationship Manager, and is scoped only to jobs posted to that one institution — it does not offer a cross-institution feed a company could self-serve subscribe to, so it does not substitute for the Apify scraper's broad coverage ([Handshake Help Center — Getting Started with EDU API](https://support.joinhandshake.com/hc/en-us/articles/31061076506391-Getting-Started-with-EDU-API)). No official data API of any kind was found for Dice, Talent.com, Jobillico, JobBank Canada, BuiltIn, or Wuzzuf in this research; for Wuzzuf specifically, the site's own `/a/API-` URL is simply a job-search results page for postings mentioning the word "API," not developer documentation ([Wuzzuf](https://wuzzuf.net/a/API-)), and BuiltIn's "job sync" feed documentation follows the same pattern as Indeed's and LinkedIn's XML feeds — built for employers pushing postings *in*, not for third parties pulling the full catalog *out* ([BuiltIn Knowledge Base — job sync feed](https://knowledgebase.builtin.com/s/article/Setting-up-your-job-sync-with-Built-In-s-standard-feed-options), page content unconfirmed due to a failed automated fetch).

## Aggregator APIs are free or cheap but don't confirm coverage of these specific 12 boards

Adzuna, Jooble, Careerjet, and JSearch are all accessible without a lengthy approval process, but each has a structural limitation that keeps it from substituting for platform-specific scraping. Adzuna's free tier (2,500 calls/month) is genuinely self-serve, but Adzuna is itself a crawling aggregator of "thousands of sources" — company career pages, staffing agencies, and niche boards — rather than a reseller of Indeed's or LinkedIn's own inventory, and no documentation confirms how much (if any) of its index overlaps with the 12 target platforms; commercial-scale use requires a custom, non-public licence negotiated directly with Adzuna ([Adzuna Developer Overview](https://developer.adzuna.com/overview)). Jooble's free API key is easy to obtain but capped at a **lifetime total of 500 requests per key** — not a monthly allowance — making it usable only for a one-time proof of concept, with no public paid-tier pricing found anywhere in its documentation ([Jooble REST API Documentation](https://help.jooble.org/en/support/solutions/articles/60001448238-rest-api-documentation)). Careerjet's API is free to access but licensed as a publisher/affiliate tool intended for embedding a Careerjet-branded search widget on a partner site in exchange for click-based revenue share, rate-limited to roughly 1,000 requests/hour — using it purely to harvest structured data rather than display Careerjet's own branded results likely sits outside the program's intended use ([Careerjet API client README](https://github.com/careerjet/careerjet-api-client-python/blob/master/README.md)). JSearch, sold on RapidAPI, is confirmed to be a wrapper that reads Google for Jobs (the same underlying source as SerpApi and DataForSEO) and aggregates roughly five upstream platforms into one response shape — so its ceiling of usefulness is the same as the Google Jobs SERP category above, not an independent data source for LinkedIn or Indeed specifically ([OpenWeb Ninja — JSearch](https://www.openwebninja.com/api/jsearch)). USAJobs is a fully free, no-approval, official government API, but its scope is strictly US federal positions, which does not overlap with any of the 12 named commercial platforms at all ([USAJOBS developer site](https://developer.usajobs.gov/)). None of these five APIs should be treated as a drop-in substitute for any of the 12 boards; at best, Adzuna and USAJobs are worth adding as supplementary, incrementally-free sources of adjacent listings Plato doesn't currently capture.

## The big scraping platforms only build pre-made products for three of twelve boards, and rarely beat Apify's price once built

Among Bright Data, Oxylabs, ScraperAPI, ScrapingBee, PhantomBuster, Coresignal, and Proxycurl, only Bright Data and Oxylabs maintain dedicated, pre-built dataset or scraper products for job listings, and even they cover just LinkedIn, Indeed, and Glassdoor directly, plus a blended multi-source "Job Postings Dataset" that bundles in ZipRecruiter, Dice, and Monster without board-specific pricing ([Bright Data LinkedIn Datasets](https://brightdata.com/products/datasets/linkedin), [Bright Data Indeed Job Posting Dataset](https://brightdata.com/products/datasets/indeed/job-posting), [Oxylabs Job Posting Dataset](https://oxylabs.io/products/datasets/job-posting)). On price, Bright Data's blog cites subscription rates of **$0.75–$0.98 per 1,000 records** for LinkedIn, Indeed, and Glassdoor jobs, with pay-as-you-go LinkedIn pricing at $1.50/1,000 ([Bright Data — Best Job APIs blog](https://brightdata.com/blog/web-data/best-job-apis), figures not independently verified against Bright Data's canonical pricing table); its general dataset marketplace floor is **$2.50 per 1,000 records** ([Bright Data Marketplace Datasets](https://brightdata.com/products/datasets/marketplace)). Oxylabs' Job Posting Dataset isn't priced per record at all — it starts at $1,000/month for a delivered dataset — while its generic Web Scraper API (the fallback for ZipRecruiter/Dice) starts around **$1.60 per 1,000 results** at entry level, ranging down to $0.25–$0.40/1,000 at volume for easier targets ([Oxylabs Web Scraper API pricing](https://oxylabs.io/products/scraper-api/web/pricing)). Set against Apify's stated $0.0007–$0.005/record ($0.70–$5.00 per 1,000), Bright Data's subscription rate for LinkedIn/Indeed/Glassdoor lands *inside* Apify's range rather than clearly below it, and only beats Apify's ceiling with subscription-refresh discounts applied. Coresignal (LinkedIn-only, still operating) prices in a wide, source-conflicted band of roughly $0.005–$0.20 per record depending on plan tier — cheap only at its higher ($800–$1,500/month) tiers, expensive at entry ([Coresignal Pricing](https://coresignal.com/pricing/); the two secondary sources used to estimate this range disagree with each other and should be verified directly). Proxycurl, the other LinkedIn specialist, has reportedly ceased operating for new customers as of mid-2025 and should be excluded from consideration entirely ([Proxycurl founder's 2026 status post](https://nubela.co/blog/what-is-proxycurl-api-now-in-2026-im-the-founder/)). ScraperAPI and ScrapingBee have no job-board-specific product at all; as generic unblocking infrastructure, real-world benchmarks put ScraperAPI at **$3.20–$8.49 per 1,000 requests** and ScrapingBee at roughly **$14.70 per 1,000 pages** once stealth proxies and JS rendering (required for most anti-bot-protected job boards) are factored in — both well above Apify's ceiling, and billed per HTTP request rather than per parsed job record, meaning true per-listing cost would run higher still ([ScraperAPI credits docs](https://docs.scraperapi.com/getting-started/quick-start/credits-and-requests-costs), [ScrapingBee credit system](https://help.scrapingbee.com/en/article/credit-system-explained-1h2ackp/)). None of the seven vendors were found to have any product — dedicated or generic-named — for Talent.com, Jobillico, JobBank Canada, BuiltIn, Handshake, or Wuzzuf; for those six boards, every one of these vendors would require a fully custom scraper built on generic proxy infrastructure, which is both more expensive per record and requires Plato to build and maintain board-specific parsing logic that a purpose-built Apify community actor already provides.

## Two low-risk wins live inside Apify itself, not in a new vendor

The cheapest, lowest-effort changes available to Plato require no new vendor relationship at all. Sampling alternative same-board Apify actors for the five niche boards found that **Jobillico** and **BuiltIn** have materially cheaper options from other authors: switching Jobillico from `jobsapi/jobillico-jobs-search-scraper` ($2.99/1,000) to `stealth_mode/jobillico-jobs-search-scraper` ($1.50/1,000) is roughly a 50% cut for the same board and comparable fields ([stealth_mode actor](https://apify.com/stealth_mode/jobillico-jobs-search-scraper/api)), and switching BuiltIn from `benthepythondev/builtin-jobs-scraper` ($3.00/1,000 Free tier) to `datascraperes/builtin-jobs-scraper` (down to $0.70/1,000 at Gold+) or `dami_studio/builtin-jobs-scraper` (flat $0.81/1,000, no tier discount) cuts cost by roughly 3–4x ([datascraperes actor](https://apify.com/datascraperes/builtin-jobs-scraper), [dami_studio actor](https://apify.com/dami_studio/builtin-jobs-scraper/api)). JobBank Canada and Wuzzuf, by contrast, are already priced at or near the cheapest of the alternatives sampled — the current Wuzzuf actor's price falls as low as $0.224/1,000 at Diamond tier, beating every competing actor checked. One outlier claim — `fetch_cat/handshake-jobs-scraper` advertising $0.02/1,000, roughly 50x cheaper than every other actor found across all five boards — is inconsistent with the pattern of every comparable listing and was confirmed by only automated page fetches, not a manual check; it should not be acted on without someone opening that actor's pricing tab directly in a browser first. Separately, Apify's own subscription tiers (Free → Starter $19/mo → Scale $199/mo → Business $999/mo) apply a Store-wide discount to every actor's per-result price, including all 12 of Plato's current job-board actors simultaneously — a broader lever than switching any single actor ([Apify Pricing](https://apify.com/pricing)). The catch is that the discount is set per-actor by each author rather than following Apify's indicative 21%/42%/56% Bronze/Silver/Gold schedule uniformly: some actors (like the Wuzzuf one) discount up to 75% at the highest tiers, while others (like `dami_studio`'s BuiltIn actor) offer 0% regardless of plan. Without Plato's actual current monthly volume per actor, the breakeven point for a tier upgrade can't be calculated from this research alone, but given a $19/month Starter tier applies across all 12 actors at once, the breakeven volume is likely to be low for any company running meaningful combined monthly volume.

## Conclusion

The research converges on a counterintuitive result for a team evaluating "alternatives to Apify": Apify's community-actor marketplace is not the expensive option here — it is, for nine of the twelve boards, the *only* practical option, because no dedicated product exists anywhere else and every generic-infrastructure fallback (ScraperAPI, ScrapingBee, Oxylabs' universal scraper) costs more once anti-bot bypass is priced in. The genuine opportunities are narrower and more surgical than "switch platforms": adopt DataForSEO for Google Jobs if the product can tolerate data that's up to a minute (Priority queue, $0.12/1,000) or up to 45 minutes (Standard queue, $0.06/1,000) behind Google's live index, swap the Jobillico and BuiltIn Apify actors for cheaper same-board alternatives after verifying field parity, and model an Apify tier upgrade against real per-actor volume rather than assuming Apify's indicative discount schedule applies uniformly. Everything gatekept behind an enterprise sales process — LinkedIn's Talent API, Indeed's post-2023 enterprise data partnership, ZipRecruiter's and Glassdoor's partner-only APIs — should be considered closed to Plato at its current scale, not merely expensive, and revisited only if a six-figure annual data budget and a multi-month procurement runway become available.
